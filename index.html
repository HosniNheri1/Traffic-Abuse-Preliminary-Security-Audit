Case study

E-commerce Platform — Traffic Abuse & Preliminary Security Audit

A WordPress / WooCommerce retail site was seeing inflated visitor statistics and unexplained load. I ran a five-day, read-only assessment to determine whether the traffic was human, automated, or mixed — and whether it exposed a genuine application vulnerability. Client and infrastructure details below are anonymized; only the methodology, evidence classes, and outcome are shown.

SCOPE
Traffic, WAF, WooCommerce, WordPress core
MODE
Read-only, non-destructive
DURATION
5-day engagement
OUTPUT
57-page report + deployed remediation

What the evidence showed

Distributed automated crawling

strongly established

Roughly a thousand requests carried no referer, spread across hundreds of unique IPs, with systematic product-taxonomy traversal and near-identical browser fingerprints — a pattern that per-IP blocking cannot address by design.

Reconnaissance scanning

confirmed

Automated probes targeted diagnostic files, credential-shaped paths, and common admin/login routes. No evidence that any probe succeeded — but the pattern alone justified blocking.

Application-function abuse

observed

Cart and product-compare actions were triggered programmatically at a steady rate, each generating real server-side processing — a resource-consumption issue distinct from simple page scraping.

Code-level vulnerabilities

none confirmed

Static review for SSRF, XSS, CSRF, SQL injection, and RCE indicators returned no matches in this preliminary pass. Reported as scoped, not as a clean bill of health — a full penetration test remains a separate, deeper engagement.

Reporting discipline: every finding was tagged as a confirmed observation, a strong indicator, or an unverified hypothesis — and the report explicitly lists what the evidence does not allow you to conclude. That distinction is what turns a scan output into a report someone can act on.

Remediation approach

Risk-managed, staged rollout

Rather than shipping a single block-list, the fix was deployed in phases — narrowest and lowest-risk first, with an observation window before anything broader, so a false positive is traceable to one specific rule.

01

Deploy the unambiguous fixes

Block requests with no legitimate use case — diagnostic-file probes, known bad crawlers — where false-positive risk is effectively zero.

02

Observe before expanding

24–48 hours of monitoring against the live customer journey — browsing, cart, checkout, login — before touching anything broader.

03

Add targeted, reversible controls

Behavioral and rate-based rules scoped to the exact abused pattern — challenge, not block, wherever a real customer could plausibly match it.

04

Record what didn't ship

Plan-level constraints and scope deviations documented as open items, not hidden — an honest as-built beats a tidy plan.

Result: six of seven planned controls active in production; the targeted behavioral rule alone classified roughly 97% of matched crawl traffic as non-human, while only a handful of genuine visitors ever saw a challenge.
